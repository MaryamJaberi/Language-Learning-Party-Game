# Test Question Designer Skill

This skill provides the theoretical and algorithmic foundation for designing challenging, authentic, and educational multiple-choice test options and reflex duels in the "دور (Dour)" language learning platform.

## Core Pedagogical Principles

### 1. Zero "Trivial" Distractors
A test is only as good as its distractors (wrong answers). If a distractor can be eliminated purely because:
- It is too short or too long compared to the correct answer.
- It is a single word while the answer is a full sentence.
- It is from an entirely irrelevant domain (e.g. "tractor" as a distractor for "good morning").
- It is grammatically incompatible (e.g. a noun when the target is a past-tense verb).
Then the learner is testing test-taking heuristics, NOT language knowledge.

### 2. Semantic Clustering (The "Same Shelf" Rule)
Distractors MUST come from the same semantic domain:
- **Food / Dining**: "Bill, please" -> "Menu, please", "Water, please", "Table for two".
- **Directions**: "Turn left at the traffic light" -> "Turn right after the bridge", "Go straight until the station", "Cross the street near the park".
- **Time / Schedule**: "What time does the store close?" -> "When does the train arrive?", "How long does the tour take?", "Is the museum open today?".
- **Emotions / Feelings**: "I am exhausted" -> "I am nervous", "I am delighted", "I am confused".

### 3. Structural & Grammatical Parity
- **Question Parity**: If the question prompt is interrogative ("Where is...?"), all 4 options must be interrogative.
- **Part of Speech**: Verbs match verbs in the exact same tense/aspect. Nouns match nouns with matching articles/gender where applicable.
- **Length & Visual Weight**: Character length within 75% to 125% of the target answer. Word counts within ±1 word.

### 4. CEFR Level Calibration
- **A1**: Basic high-frequency contrasts (e.g. days of week, colors, basic pronouns, numbers).
- **A2**: Common daily routines, prepositions of place/time, common irregular past forms.
- **B1**: Modal verbs, connector words, conditional phrasing, telephone/email etiquette.
- **B2**: Nuanced synonyms, false friends, phrasal verbs, idiomatic business expressions.
- **C1 / C2**: Complex register differences (formal vs colloquial), stylistic nuances, rhetorical phrases.
